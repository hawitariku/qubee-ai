The Complete Guide to the Qubee Alphabet

June 2026 · 8 min read · Qubeessaa AI Blog
← Back to Blog

The Qubee alphabet is the official writing system of Afaan Oromo, one of the most widely spoken languages in Africa. Adopted in 1991, Qubee replaced earlier scripts and gave the Oromo people a standardized, accessible way to write their language using Latin characters. Today, millions of people across Ethiopia, Kenya, and the wider Oromo diaspora use Qubee every day in schools, government offices, newspapers, and digital communication.

Understanding Qubee is the first and most important step toward writing Afaan Oromo correctly. This guide covers everything you need to know: the basic alphabet, vowel length distinctions, digraphs, and how each letter maps to actual Oromo speech sounds.

What Is Qubee?

Qubee (pronounced "qu-BEY") literally means "letter" or "alphabet" in Afaan Oromo. The script was developed by a team of Oromo linguists and scholars, most notably Dr. Tilahun Gamta, who worked to create a writing system that would accurately represent the sounds of Afaan Oromo using the familiar Latin alphabet.

Before Qubee, Afaan Oromo was written in the Ethiopic script (Ge'ez/Fidel), which was designed for Amharic and Tigrinya. Because Afaan Oromo has several sounds that do not exist in those languages, the Ethiopic script was a poor fit. Qubee solved this problem by adapting Latin characters to precisely represent every sound in Afaan Oromo.

Key fact: Qubee was officially adopted by the Oromia Regional State in 1991 and is now the primary script used in all Afaan Oromo education, media, and government in Ethiopia.

Vowel Length: A Critical Distinction

One of the most important features of Qubee — and one of the most common sources of spelling errors — is vowel length. In Afaan Oromo, how long you hold a vowel changes the meaning of the word entirely. Short vowels are written with a single letter; long vowels are written by doubling the letter.

Afaan Oromo uses several digraphs — pairs of letters that together represent a single sound. These are not two separate sounds; they must always be treated as one unit.

Geminate Consonants

Just like vowels can be long (doubled), consonants can also be geminated (doubled) in Afaan Oromo. A doubled consonant is held slightly longer during speech and changes the meaning of a word. For example:

  • Ala (outside/wilderness) vs. Alla (God — informal/dialectal)
  • Ati (you) vs. Atti (how you / the way you)
  • Baga (welcome/congratulations) vs. Bagga (bank of a river)

Geminate consonants are a major source of spelling errors, especially for people learning to write Afaan Oromo after speaking it informally for years. The doubling must be represented in writing because it carries meaning.

Letters Unique to Qubee

While Qubee uses the standard Latin alphabet, several letters represent sounds that are unique to Afaan Oromo and do not exist in English:

  • Q — A uvular consonant, produced further back in the throat than the English "k". It is the sound at the start of the word Qubee itself.
  • X — An emphatic "t" with additional pharyngeal constriction. It sounds like a "t" produced with extra tension at the back of the throat.
  • Dh — An implosive consonant where air flows inward rather than outward during production. This sound is found in words like Dhugaa (truth) and Dhiiga (blood).
  • Ph — A heavily aspirated "p", more forceful than the English "p".

Tips for Learning Qubee

  • Start with vowel sounds. Afaan Oromo vowels are pure and consistent — unlike English where "a" can sound different in "cat", "cake", and "call". Once you know the 5 Oromo vowels, they always sound the same.
  • Practice vowel length every day. It is the hardest part for beginners. Read aloud and pay attention to whether words should be short or long.
  • Learn digraphs as single units. Do not try to say each letter separately in dh or ny — they are one sound.
  • Use a spell checker. Tools like Qubeessaa AI will flag vowel length errors and incorrect consonant doubling, helping you learn from your mistakes.
  • Read Afaan Oromo texts regularly. Newspapers, books, and websites in Afaan Oromo will reinforce correct spelling patterns through repetition.

Conclusion

The Qubee alphabet is elegant in its design. By using familiar Latin letters with a small number of well-defined modifications, it provides a complete and accurate writing system for Afaan Oromo. The keys to mastering it are understanding vowel length, recognizing digraphs as single sounds, and being aware of geminate consonants.
