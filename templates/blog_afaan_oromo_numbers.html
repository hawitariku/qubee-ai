Numbers, Counting, and Math in Afaan Oromo

June 2026 · 7 min read · Qubeessaa AI Blog
← Back to Blog

Knowing how to count and express numbers is a fundamental part of any language. In Afaan Oromo, the number system is logical and consistent — once you learn the basic numbers, you can construct any larger number using simple patterns. This guide covers cardinal numbers (one, two, three…), ordinal numbers (first, second, third…), how to express dates and time, and basic arithmetic terms in Afaan Oromo.

Pattern: For compound numbers, Afaan Oromo places the larger unit first, then the smaller unit. "35" = Soddomaa fi shan (thirty and five). The word fi (and) connects the units.

Expressing Time

Time in Afaan Oromo is expressed using the word sa'aatii (hour/clock). Note that Ethiopia traditionally uses a 12-hour clock starting at dawn (6 AM = 12 in Ethiopian time), but modern Afaan Oromo writing often uses the standard international system.

  • "Sa'aatii meeqa?" — What time is it?
  • "Sa'aatii tokko" — One o'clock
  • "Sa'aatii kudha lama daqiiqaa soddomaa" — 12:30
  • "Ganama" — Morning
  • "Guyyaa" — Daytime/afternoon
  • "Galgala" — Evening
  • "Halkan" — Night
Spelling note: Number words in Afaan Oromo have specific spellings that must be memorized. Common mistakes include writing Toko instead of Tokko (missing geminate consonant) or lama vs lamaa. Use the Qubeessaa AI spell checker to verify number word spellings.

Conclusion

Afaan Oromo's number system is consistent and logical. The base-ten structure, the compound number patterns, and the ordinal suffix system all follow clear rules that make them learnable. Knowing the months, days, and time expressions gives you practical tools for everyday written communication. Whether you are writing a date on a form, describing a quantity, or writing about mathematics in school, mastering Afaan Oromo numbers is an essential foundation.
